The Story of Ivalice

Set in the medieval fantasy world of Ivalice, Final Fantasy Tactics tells the tale of Ramza Beoulve, the third son of Balbanes Beoulve, who is caught in a web of political intrigue, war, and betrayal. Following the death of King Omdoria of Ivalice, a succession crisis erupts as rival noble houses—led by Duke Larg and Duke Goltanna—contest control of the kingdom. Ramza is thrust into a struggle that will determine the fate of the entire nation.

The game follows Ramza's journey as he uncovers a conspiracy involving the Church of Glabados, the Order of the Northern Sky, and the mysterious Heretics. Along the way, he forms an unlikely alliance with Delita Heiral, a commoner with ambitions of his own, and together they navigate the treacherous politics of Ivalice.

Key Characters

Ramza Beoulve

The third son of Balbanes Beoulve, Ramza is a skilled warrior who values justice and honor above all else. His idealistic nature often puts him at odds with the corrupt nobility around him.

Delita Heiral

A commoner and Ramza's childhood friend, Delita is ambitious and resourceful. While Ramza fights for justice, Delita seeks power and recognition, leading to a complex and evolving relationship between the two.

Olan Durai

Olan Durai is an Astrologist serving the southern order who encounters Ramza during the story.

Alma Beoulve

Ramza's younger sister, Alma is a Cleric who uses the MBarrier spell to protect her allies. Her gentle nature and protective instincts drive much of the game's central conflict.

Tactical Battles

Final Fantasy Tactics revolutionized the genre with its deep tactical combat system. Battles take place on a three-dimensional grid, where players must carefully position their units to gain tactical advantages. Terrain, elevation, and weather all play crucial roles in determining the outcome of engagements.

Each unit has a unique set of abilities and stats that grow as they gain experience. Players must strategically assign jobs to characters, allowing them to access different skill sets and adapt to various combat situations. The depth of the job system means that nearly any character can be customized to fit any role on the battlefield.

The game features approximately 70 fixed encounters across the story and optional content. Additional repeatable random battles provide opportunities to train your units.

The Job System

One of the most celebrated features of Final Fantasy Tactics is its innovative job system. Each character has one current job at a time, which determines their available abilities, stats, and equipment. In addition, characters can equip a secondary action-ability set learned from another job, plus learned reaction, support, and movement abilities, allowing for deep customization over time.

The original PlayStation game features 20 standard jobs, ranging from classic roles like Knight and White Mage to more specialized classes like Time Mage and Geomancer. Some jobs, such as Knight and White Mage, are available to all characters, while others like Dark Knight are restricted to specific characters. Each job has its own progression tree, with abilities that can be unlocked as the character gains experience in that class.

The job system encourages experimentation and creative team building. Players can swap jobs between battles to adapt their characters to different roles, such as pairing a Knight with a Monk for a versatile melee fighter. Unlike later RPGs, characters cannot equip multiple jobs simultaneously—their stat sets are determined by their currently equipped job, but they can also equip supplementary learned abilities from other jobs.

Original PlayStation Release

Final Fantasy Tactics was originally released for the Sony PlayStation in Japan on June 20, 1997, and in North America in January 1998. Developed and published by Square, the game was directed by Yasumi Matsuno and produced by Hironobu Sakaguchi.

The game was a critical and commercial success, praised for its deep story, complex gameplay mechanics, and stunning presentation for the era. It has since been re-released on multiple platforms, including the PlayStation Portable, iOS, Android, and various modern PC and console platforms.

The original PlayStation version combines pixel-art character sprites with polygonal battlefield environments, giving the game a unique visual identity that remains iconic among RPG fans.
